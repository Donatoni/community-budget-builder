Understanding Amendment 3

A closer look at what is changing.

Florida voters approved Amendment 3 on the November 3, 2026 statewide ballot. It changes how homestead property is taxed for non-school services.

A larger exemption, phased in over time

The 2026 exemption is up to approximately $51,411. The amendment increases the non-school portion and lowers the assessment cap for non-homestead property.

Why it matters locally Police, fire, streets, parks, planning, and city operations rely on non-school property taxes.
  1. 01
    $25,000 school-tax exemption

    The school-tax portion remains capped at $25,000.

  2. 02
    Up to $150,000 in 2027

    Non-school homestead exemption in the first year.

  3. 03
    Up to $250,000 in 2028

    Annual inflation adjustments begin in 2029.

  4. 04
    Assessment cap reduced to 5%

    For non-school assessments of non-homestead property, down from 10%. This caps assessed-value growth, not tax rates.

  5. 05
    A process for future exemption increases

    The Legislature will establish a process for counties and cities to increase exemptions further. The amendment does not set a date for full elimination.

Eligibility depends on homestead status and Florida residency. New residents arriving in 2027 or later follow a different phase-in. Read the Volusia County Property Appraiser’s Amendment 3 FAQ for details.

Public budget meetings

Join the Budget Conversation

The City of DeLand believes our community should have every opportunity to understand the challenges ahead and participate in the decisions that will shape City services. That is why we have expanded our public budget meeting process to discuss the impact of Amendment 3 on the City’s FY 2027/28 budget.

Nov. 9, 2026 · City Commission special meeting

Where things stand

During the Nov. 9 meeting, the Commission reviewed potential revenue options for FY 2027/28 and directed staff to research several of them further. These options are only being studied; no decisions have been made about whether to implement any new revenue sources.

Agenda and packet ↗ (opens in a new tab) Watch on the meeting portal ↗ (opens in a new tab)

What’s next

Upcoming meetings will examine the costs of City services, potential changes to those services, and options for balancing the budget. Here is the schedule:

  1. Jan 11

    Review of required services and cost centers for Public Works, Parks and Recreation, and Community Development.

  2. Feb 8

    Review of required services and cost centers for Public Safety and Administrative Support Services.

  3. Mar 22

    Review of employee pay and benefits, buyout options, and early retirement options.

  4. Apr 12

    Review of legal requirements, refined revenue estimates, implementation costs, equity impacts, and the scope and timing of necessary studies.

  5. Apr 26

    Discussion of preferred revenue options and potential service changes to balance the budget.

  6. May 24

    Final policy direction.

When and where All meetings will begin at 6 p.m. at City Hall (120 S. Florida Ave., DeLand, FL), and audio will be streamed online. Agendas, live streams, and recordings are posted on the City’s meeting portal (opens in a new tab).

Residents are encouraged to attend, ask questions, and learn more about the services your tax dollars support. Your participation matters as we work through these difficult decisions together.
